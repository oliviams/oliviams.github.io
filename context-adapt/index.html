ContextAdapt

Evaluating Contextual Adaptation and Value Alignment in LLMs
The same value can call for different behaviour in different domains. Do LLMs adapt accordingly?

ContextAdapt tests whether LLMs apply honesty, autonomy and confidentiality the way each profession actually does, across medicine, law, finance and national security, and whether they stay consistent when context changes but the professional rule does not.

01 · THE IDEA

One value, different rules in different professions.

Honesty, autonomy and confidentiality sound like universal principles, but each profession spells out what they require in practice: who the duty is owed to, how far it extends, and when it can be set aside. To illustrate, here is how honesty is operationalised differently across two of our four domains, medicine and law.

Honesty in Medicine

Honesty can license withholding

Medical guidance permits a documented exception to full disclosure, therapeutic privilege, when disclosure itself would seriously harm the patient. The exception protects the very person the duty of honesty is owed to.

Duty owed to: the patient
Honesty in Law

Honesty runs to the court first

A lawyer cannot knowingly mislead the court, and owes the client no equivalent duty to volunteer an uncomfortable truth. Candour runs first to the court, and only second to the client.

Duty owed to: the court, then the client

The same value therefore points in different directions in different settings. A model that applies one rule everywhere may look consistent but miss real differences; one that changes behaviour whenever context changes is not necessarily right either. Calibrated contextual adaptation means adapting when context alters the governing norm, and staying consistent when it does not.

For every value × domain pair we identify three things

Default rule

How the value is operationalised in that profession, drawn from 24 primary-source professional and regulatory documents.

Target of the duty

Who or what the obligation is owed to: a client, a patient, a court, an institution or a source.

Exception conditions

When the default may be overridden. Each exception is probed where it applies, where it does not, and at the boundary.

What “appropriate” means here. A response is appropriate if it corresponds to the professional operationalisation in the source corpus (mainly UK, US and international institutions). It is not a claim of context-independent moral truth, and stated justifications describe the framework a response expresses, not the model's internal reasoning.

05 · WHAT IT MEANS

Evaluate adaptation and invariance.

Aggregate accuracy is not enough

High average appropriateness does not show a value has been robustly learned: models can reach the same action through different frameworks, and fail sharply under changes that leave the rule unchanged.

Test both directions

Evaluations of abstract values should test adaptation (behaviour changes when the governing norm changes) and invariance (it does not when the norm is unchanged).

Principles may need structure

Principle-based alignment may need richer specification of roles, targets, thresholds and exceptions, or models that infer this structure reliably from context.
