ContextAdapt

Evaluating Contextual Adaptation and Value Alignment in LLMs
The same value can call for different behaviour in different domains. Do LLMs adapt accordingly?

ContextAdapt tests whether LLMs apply honesty, autonomy and confidentiality the way each profession actually does, across medicine, law, finance and national security, and whether they stay consistent when context changes but the professional rule does not.

01 · THE IDEA

One value, different rules in different professions.

We generally think of honesty, autonomy and confidentiality as universal principles, but what they require in practice varies across different professions: who the duty is owed to, how far it extends, and when it can be set aside. To illustrate, here is how honesty is operationalised differently across two of our four domains, medicine and law.

Honesty in Medicine

Honesty can license withholding

Medical guidance permits a documented exception to full disclosure (therapeutic privilege) when disclosure itself would seriously harm the patient. The exception protects the very person the duty of honesty is owed to.

Duty owed to: the patient
Honesty in Law

Honesty runs to the court first

A lawyer cannot knowingly mislead the court, and owes the client no equivalent duty to volunteer an uncomfortable truth. Candour runs first to the court, and only second to the client.

Duty owed to: the court, then the client

The same value therefore points in different directions in different settings. A model that applies one rule everywhere may look consistent but miss real differences; one that changes behaviour whenever context changes is not necessarily right either. Calibrated contextual adaptation means adapting when context alters the governing norm, and staying consistent when it does not.

For every value × domain pair we identify three things

Default rule

How the value is operationalised in that profession, drawn from primary-source professional and regulatory documents.

Target of the duty

Who or what the obligation is owed to: a client, a patient, a court, an institution or a source.

Exception conditions

When the default may be overridden. Each exception is probed where it applies, where it does not, and at the boundary.

04 · SECONDARY EXPERIMENT

Do changes in context matter?

Explicitly naming the profession or changing the model's role from decision-maker to advisor barely matters. Changing the stakes, without changing the professional rule, does.

05 · ROBUSTNESS

Do the findings depend on the judge?

Every response is coded by three LLM judges. Recomputing the headline statistics with each judge alone, the main findings hold; absolute justification rates, and one striking example, do not.

The main findings holdUnder every judge, main-dataset appropriateness stays near ceiling (93.1–96.8%), the stakes effect is 9–11 points, and role and label effects are at most 1.3 points.
Orderings are stable, absolute rates are notAll three judges rank Gemma 2 27B lowest and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite highest on correct professional grounding (Spearman 0.89–0.98 between judges), but Command A counts 28.9% of responses as correctly grounded against about 50% for the other two.
Robust to compositionDropping any one domain, template or scenario family leaves the stakes effect between +7 and +12 points. Without national security: 96.4% appropriateness, 53.7% correct grounding, and a +7.6-point stakes effect.

Judge caveats. Pairwise agreement between judges is modest (Cohen’s κ 0.34–0.58 for the action, Krippendorff’s α 0.22–0.51 for the justification, across both experiments), and the labels come from LLM judges only, with no human annotation. All three judges are also evaluated models: Command A scores its own model’s responses 5–7 points higher on appropriateness than its usual scoring would predict, consistent with a possible own-model scoring effect that the panel consensus reduces but cannot rule out.
06 · WHAT IT MEANS

Evaluating value alignment means testing both adaptation and consistency.

Aggregate accuracy is not enough

High average appropriateness does not show a value has been robustly learned: models can reach the same action through different frameworks, and fail sharply under changes that leave the rule unchanged.

Test both directions

Evaluations of abstract values should test adaptation (behaviour changes when the governing norm changes) and consistency (it does not when the norm is unchanged).

Grounding is observable, with limits

Separating the action from the stated justification makes differences in professional grounding visible, though stated justifications are not faithful accounts of a model’s internal reasoning.
