Framing the Narrative:
Ideological Mimicry in Large Language Models

Political stance in LLMs is interaction-dependent.

Poli‑SHIFT is a dataset and evaluation framework for testing whether signals about the user's political stance systematically move the political stance an LLM expresses.

Poli‑SHIFT holds the underlying policy question fixed and varies one interaction signal at a time. Pick a treatment, then pick a variant, to see the typical direction a model's stance moves (averaged across all seven models).

Paper summary

What the study argues.

Read the abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to answer questions about politically contentious issues, yet evaluations typically treat a model's stance as a relatively stable property. Real users, however, communicate political signals through their terminology, assumptions, and personal context. We investigate whether such signals produce ideological mimicry: systematic shifts in the political stance expressed by an LLM toward the position conveyed by the interaction. We build the Poli-SHIFT dataset and evaluation framework and assess seven open-weight LLMs across ten contentious political topics in the United States, United Kingdom, and Australia, systematically manipulating contested terminology, politically valenced premises, and user information, and eliciting responses in both multiple-choice and open-text formats. Across models, we find robust evidence that prompt framing shapes the political stance of LLM outputs — and that stated political ideology systematically shifts responses toward the user's own position.
